\section{Conclusion}

We asked a simple question that existing benchmarks cannot answer: when told to look further ahead, does a language model choose differently, and better? With the state held fixed and an exact answer at every horizon, current open models split into three tiers. Small models ignore the horizon. Larger models abandon the obvious move. The two strongest reasoning models also find the long-horizon move more often, gpt-oss on both characters and Qwen3-32B reliably on one. Yet their answers do not get better on average, because they also give up correct answers. A model that changes its answer when told to plan has not necessarily planned. Telling the two apart needs a test that checks the plan against an exact answer. The protocol here is one such test, and it transfers to any domain with a simulator.
